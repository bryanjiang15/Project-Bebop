# Project Bebop – greybox art, draft v0.2 (pending Bryan's review)

Greybox placeholders for the terraforming 4X prototype (Godot 4, isometric 2D on a hex grid).
Everything is generated by `src/make_art.py` (assets) and `src/make_preview.py` (preview).
To rebuild: `cd src && python3 make_art.py && python3 make_preview.py` (Python 3 + Pillow).
**Not final.** Every size, color, and convention below is a draft until Bryan approves it.

## Hex grid and projection
- **Pointy-top hexes** (Civ 6 convention; the brief says "Civ 6-style hex grid" but doesn't give an orientation).
- **v0.2 (higher camera, per Bryan's review of v0.1):** a true top-down pointy hex 128 px wide is about 148 px tall. Here the face is **128×108** (height/width 0.84, i.e. about 0.73 of top-down; v0.1 was 128×72). I chose 108 because a quarter of it (27) and three quarters (81) are whole pixels, and no pixel center lands exactly on a hex edge, so the tiles tessellate exactly.
- Ground squash K ≈ 0.73 (the 108 face height divided by the 148 px top-down height). Heights on decorations, units and cities scale by about 0.68 (√(1−K²)).
- Top-face vertices, in tile pixels: (64,0) (128,27) (128,81) (64,108) (0,81) (0,27).
- **Grid spacing:** column step 128 px; row step **81 px** (¾ of 108); odd rows shift right by 64 px.
- **Side thickness:** **8 px** below the lower edges (v0.1 was 16), darker on the left half (light comes from the upper right). On a flat map the next row covers it, so it only shows on the bottom and side edges of the map or next to holes in the map. All tiles share one ground height, so they always interlock.
- The face is rasterized by testing pixel centers, so tiles tessellate exactly. The preview script checks this: **0 overlap pixels and 0 gap pixels.**

## Godot 4 setup
**TileSet** (on a `TileMapLayer`):
- `tile_shape = TileSet.TILE_SHAPE_HEXAGON`
- `tile_layout = TileSet.TILE_LAYOUT_STACKED` (odd rows offset)
- `tile_offset_axis = TileSet.TILE_OFFSET_AXIS_HORIZONTAL` (this is pointy-top)
- `tile_size = Vector2i(128, 108)`. Set it to the **face** size, not the PNG size.

**TileSetAtlasSource:**
- `texture = tile_sheet.png`
- `texture_region_size = Vector2i(128, 116)`, margins 0, separation 0
- For each tile, set `texture_origin = Vector2i(0, -4)` so the 108 px face is centered on the cell and the 8 px side hangs below it. Check this once in the TileSet editor: the face should line up with the hex grid outline. If it sits 8 px too high or too low, flip the sign.
- Use a second TileMapLayer above the terrain for the highlight (atlas coords (5,0)), or a Sprite2D.

**Picking:** `layer.local_to_map(layer.get_local_mouse_position())` maps to the face, because `tile_size` is the face.

**Unit and city anchors (pivots):**
| Sprite | Canvas | Anchor (ground point) | Sprite2D with `centered = true` |
|---|---|---|---|
| unit_* | 64×72 | (32, 56), the center of the base disc | `offset = Vector2(0, -20)` |
| city_* | 128×128 | (64, 74), the center of the tile face (the face spans y 20–128 of the canvas) | `offset = Vector2(0, -10)` |

Put the sprite at `position = terrain_layer.map_to_local(cell)`, which is the cell center. Put units and cities under a `Node2D` with `y_sort_enabled = true` so lower rows draw over higher ones. If a tile holds a city and a unit, nudge the unit about 24 px toward the lower right. Units and cities can extend up over the tile behind them. That's intentional, and y-sort handles it.

**Team color:** in the art, the grey `#B8B8B8` areas (unit base ring and badge band, city roofs) are where team color goes. Recolor them with the mask:
```glsl
shader_type canvas_item;
uniform sampler2D team_mask;
uniform vec4 team_color : source_color = vec4(1.0);
void fragment() {
    vec4 c = texture(TEXTURE, UV);
    float m = texture(team_mask, UV).a;
    c.rgb = mix(c.rgb, clamp(c.rgb * team_color.rgb * (255.0 / 184.0), 0.0, 1.0), m);
    COLOR = c;
}
```
A simpler option: add a child Sprite2D with the mask texture and `modulate = team_color` (the shading is flatter).

## Palette (flat, muted; also told apart by lightness and pattern)
| Land | Face | Detail / pattern | CIE L* |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plains | `#C8C27A` | `#9A9555` short grass ticks | 77 (lightest) |
| Hills | `#B09269` | `#745C3C` two low domes from above (lit cap upper right) | 62 |
| Water | `#4E7598` | `#8FB6D3` wave marks | 48 |
| Forest | `#385E3B` | `#27452B` round tree canopies from above, with shadows | 36 |
| Volcanic ground | `#3A3032` | `#D05A2C` glowing cracks | 21 (darkest) |

Lightness steps are about 14 L* apart, and each land type has its own pattern, so the map still reads in grayscale (checked).
- Sides: face × 0.55 (left) and × 0.70 (right). Face edge rim: face × 0.82. Outlines: `#222222`.
- Units: body `#ECECEC`, side `#A8A8A8`, glyph `#222222`, team area `#B8B8B8`.
- Cities: walls `#D8D8D8` and `#B4B4B4`, plaza `#9C968C`, roofs `#B8B8B8` (team area).
- Preview team colors (examples only): blue `#3D7EDB`, red `#D94A3D`.

## Unit silhouettes
Each unit is a standee badge on a short post and a low base disc (v0.2: post 10 px, base 36×24 px with 3 px thickness). The badge shape, the glyph, and a letter tag each identify the unit:
- **Settler:** circle, house glyph, "Se" (civilian)
- **Scout:** diamond, double chevron (fast), "Sc"
- **Warrior:** heater shield, sword, "W"
- **Archer:** triangle, bow and arrow, "A"

## Cities
- `city_village`: 3 houses on a small hex plaza (0.40 of the tile face). For a newly founded city.
- `city_town`: 7 houses and a central keep on a larger hex plaza (0.55 of the face). For a grown city.
- v0.2 houses are viewed from above: a hip roof (team tint) plus a short front wall.
The brief has population growth but no visual stages. I picked the switch point as a suggestion only: show town at population 4 or more.

## Where each convention came from
- **From the brief, design doc, and tasks:** Godot; isometric 2D on a hex grid; Civ 6-style map; greybox flat colors with one color per land type; the 5 land types (plains, forest, hills, water, volcanic ground); units are settler, scout, warrior, and archer; cities sit on tiles; colored territory borders and city name banners exist (banners are left to the UI; not drawn here).
- **Picked by me (draft):** pointy-top orientation; 128×108 face and 8 px side (v0.2; Bryan asked for a higher camera, and the exact pixels are mine); the Godot TileSet values above; anchors; the palette; the standee unit design and team-tint masks; the two city stages; file names.
